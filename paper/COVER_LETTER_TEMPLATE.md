**"When Static Thresholds Fail: Online Conformal Calibration for Cross-Domain Industrial Anomaly Detection"**

by Hu Lingyan, Zhu Jibiao (School of Electronic and Electrical Engineering, Shanghai University of Engineering Science, Shanghai, China).

**Contribution in brief.** Industrial anomaly detectors are deployed through decision thresholds, yet cross-domain studies focus on ranking quality. We present a six-fold, four-domain-family benchmark (semiconductor CMP, wind, solar, rotating machinery) at native sampling rates, and show that a static source-calibrated threshold fails in *both* directions under domain shift (normal-window coverage 0.18--0.999 at a nominal 0.90), while an online adaptive-conformal layer restores coverage to within 0.015 on five of six folds at 0.6--0.8 microseconds per decision. We report negative results honestly (divergence-shifted and conditional variants add no gain; a divergence statistic does not predict transfer cost) and state explicitly that calibration cannot rescue a below-chance ranker. The paper also verifies the findings with two classical score producers, showing the calibration conclusions are not encoder-specific.

**Fit to TIM.** The work studies the measurement/reliability layer of industrial monitoring---calibration, false-alarm control, detection delay, runtime---with reproducible protocols, released code, and a practical deployment guideline, which aligns with TIM's scope in instrumentation and measurement for industrial systems.
