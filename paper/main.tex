\title{Cross-Domain Industrial Anomaly Detection:\\
When Static Thresholds Fail and Online Conformal Calibration Restores Coverage}

\author{Hu~Lingyan and Zhu~Jibiao

\begin{abstract}
Industrial anomaly detectors are typically trained and calibrated on one operating domain, then deployed on another. We study what happens to the deployed \emph{decision threshold} under this cross-domain shift. Using six leave-one-group-out folds spanning semiconductor fabrication, wind, solar, and rotating-machinery monitoring at native sampling rates, we show that a static source-calibrated threshold fails in both directions at a nominal 0.90 normal-window coverage level: coverage ranges from $0.18$ (severe over-alarming) to $0.999$ (no alarms). An online adaptive conformal inference (ACI) layer restores coverage to within $0.015$ of nominal on five of six folds across four nominal levels, with bootstrap confidence intervals for the coverage gap of at most $0.02$; rolling-quantile and extreme-value baselines do not recover coverage. We then identify an \emph{alarm-budget defect}: ACI regulates the stream-level alarm rate, so on folds where anomalies are abundant and separable the budget is consumed by anomalies and normal-window coverage over-shoots. A normal-anchored variant (NA-ACI) that updates the quantile only on likely-normal windows removes the defect---detection rate on the machinery fold rises from $0.12$ to $0.72$ at matched normal-window FPR, and mean range-$F_1$ across folds improves from $0.26$ to $0.39$---while matching ACI on anomaly-sparse folds. Conditional (Mondrian) and divergence-shifted variants otherwise bring no measurable coverage gain, and a band-spectral divergence estimate does not predict the measured transfer cost (interval inflation $0.5\times$--$19\times$). Calibration and detection quality decouple: on two folds with below-chance score rankings, coverage remains controllable but alarms carry no detection utility---a caveat we state explicitly rather than hide behind the coverage numbers. We release the benchmark, labeling and injection protocols, and code, along with a deployment guideline.
\end{abstract}
