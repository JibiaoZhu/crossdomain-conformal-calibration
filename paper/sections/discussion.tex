\section{Discussion and Deployment Guideline}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What We Would Tell a Practitioner}
Four rules follow directly from the study. (1) \emph{Never ship a source-calibrated static threshold for a new domain.} Across four domain families the static threshold failed on six of six folds and in both directions; the failure is not a tuning problem but a distributional one. (2) \emph{Wrap the score with an online ACI layer and monitor its trace; use NA-ACI when anomalies may be abundant.} On five folds the layer recovered coverage within $0.015$ of nominal at negligible cost ($0.6$--$0.8\,\mu$s per decision); on anomaly-rich streams, NA-ACI keeps the budget on the normal regime and improves detection sharply at matched FPR, at the price of one interpretable parameter $\kappa$ whose exclusion rate is monitorable online. (3) \emph{Report coverage and alarm rate separately, and know which one is guaranteed.} ACI controls the long-run alarm rate; normal-window FPR control follows when anomalies are rare relative to $\alphaNom$, and NA-ACI narrows the gap otherwise. (4) \emph{Audit the ranker before trusting the calibration.} Two folds here rank anomalies below chance; their coverage numbers look excellent and their alarms are worthless. A feature-baseline AUROC check (RMS or spectral energy) costs nothing and exposes this.

\subsection{Limitations}
The benchmark has six folds over four domain families, with single-dataset wind and one fab dataset; "cross-domain" here means cross-population within industrial monitoring, not six separate industries. Pseudo-labels are unavoidable for several datasets, and PHM16's $q_{10}$ removal-rate protocol and the PVDAQ synthetic injection are the weakest links; both are documented and their sensitivity is reported (contamination sweep, diagnosis of below-chance folds). The encoder family is deliberately simple and uniform; we compare against mechanism-level reimplementations of the closest rivals (CICADA-, DACAD-style, Section~\ref{sec:rivals}) and against classical and foundation-model score producers (PCA, band-energy Mahalanobis, Chronos-bolt-small), while official-code comparisons remain a revision item. Finally, the conditional-coverage goal remains open: Mondrian and divergence-shifted variants did not deliver it, and NA-ACI narrows but does not close the conditional gap, so we claim no more than marginal coverage control with an improved budget allocation.

\subsection{Future Work}
Three directions follow. First, conditional coverage beyond marginal control: NA-ACI fixes the allocation of the alarm budget but does not provide group-conditional guarantees, and the assumption behind its exclusion rule (anomaly-pure tail) deserves theory that links measurable diagnostics---exclusion rate, score-distribution drift---to coverage bounds. Second, extending the benchmark with more domain families and externally produced scores (foundation models, domain-adaptation detectors) to test whether the calibration conclusions and NA-ACI gains transfer to other score producers. Third, a theory-side account of interval inflation: inflation spans two orders of magnitude across our folds and we can describe it but not yet predict it.
