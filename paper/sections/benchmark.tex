\section{Problem Setup and Benchmark}
\label{sec:benchmark}

\subsection{Setting and Metrics}
\label{sec:setup-metrics}
A detector produces a window-level anomaly score $\st$ from a multivariate window $\xt$ (window length 256 samples, stride 128, native sampling rates throughout). Training uses \emph{source-normal} windows only, pooled across source domains. At deployment, the target stream is unlabeled; we may use an initial segment as an unlabeled calibration stream but never its labels. A calibration rule turns scores into thresholds $\qt$ and alarms $\mathbb{1}[\st > \qt]$.

We report the following quantities. \emph{Normal-window coverage} $\cover = 1-\FPR$ is the fraction of evaluation windows with label ``normal'' that are not alarmed, with the requirement $\cover \approx 1-\alphaNom$ at nominal level $\alphaNom$; unless stated otherwise, coverage is the calibration target and all calibration tables report it. The \emph{coverage gap} is $\gap = |\cover-(1-\alphaNom)|$. We also report the \emph{alarm rate} over the whole stream, because an online rule that regulates alarms over a stream containing anomalies cannot in general also control the normal-window FPR: the two coincide only when anomalies are rare relative to $\alphaNom$. \emph{Threshold inflation} is $\overline{\qt}/\qsrc$, the mean deployed threshold relative to the source-calibrated quantile; it measures the transfer cost of keeping coverage. Detection-side metrics are range-based $F_1$, AUROC/AUPRC, false alarms per window (equivalently per 1000 windows), and detection delay in windows after an anomaly run begins. Window duration is set in samples (256 at stride 128), so its physical span varies with the native rate---from $4.3$\,min on F1 (1\,Hz) to $5$--$21$\,ms on the kHz folds; we report physical-time equivalents where absolute timing matters. Notation: $\cover$ is normal-window coverage ($1-\FPR$); $1-\alphaNom$ is the nominal coverage target; \emph{alarm rate} is the fraction of \emph{all} evaluation windows alarmed; \emph{threshold inflation} is the deployed-to-source threshold ratio; every reported quantity names its measurement domain (normal windows vs whole stream). All calibration results are averaged over three training seeds. For uncertainty we use hierarchical moving-block bootstrap: evaluation windows are resampled in contiguous blocks within each fold to respect temporal dependence, and block-length sensitivity (50/200/1{,}000 windows) is reported.

\subsection{Six Folds over Four Domain Families}
\label{sec:folds}
We construct six leave-one-group-out folds (each fold holds out one dataset group; four domain families are covered). Table~\ref{tab:datasets} lists targets, native rates, evaluation set sizes, anomaly fractions, and label provenance; sources for each fold are all other groups' normal windows. Two guardrails apply throughout: folds whose ranking quality is below chance are excluded from detection-quality claims (diagnosed in Section~\ref{sec:decoupling}), and the synthetic-injection fold (F3) is flagged in every table and figure caption. Two dataset-engineering decisions follow from pilot experiments and are fixed throughout: (i) windows are standardized with per-dataset global statistics---per-window standardization removes the amplitude component of degradation and we observed it destroy ranking on run-to-failure data; (ii) no resampling to a common rate is performed, so spectral differences between domains are preserved.

\subsection{Labeling and Injection Protocols}
\label{sec:protocols}
Pseudo-labeling is unavoidable in parts of this benchmark, and each protocol is a documented limitation. PHM16-CMP \cite{phm2016} has no fault labels; we mark a wafer-stage run anomalous when its removal rate falls in the lowest per-stage decile ($q_{10}$), giving an 11.6\% anomaly fraction. CARE wind \cite{care2024} carries status-based anomaly labels that we use directly. PVDAQ \cite{pvdaq} contains normal-operation data only; the F3 fold evaluates a synthetic injection protocol---$+6\sigma$ spikes, $+4\sigma$ level shifts, and segment dropouts applied to 20\% of evaluation windows with a fixed seed---and is reported as synthetic-only. Dropouts zero a contiguous span (one-eighth to one-third of the window, both channels) in the standardized window and are scored like any other window, with no masking. Band-spectral features partition the normalized frequency axis ($0$--Nyquist) into eight equal-width rFFT power bins computed on globally standardized windows, so band comparisons are relative-frequency and amplitude-excluded by construction. CWRU supplies seeded-fault files (label normal/fault). Paderborn \cite{paderborn} is the time-varying run-to-failure release (bearings B01--B04 at 128\,kHz; $\sim$1.6\,s measurements accumulated over bearing life)---distinct from the classic fixed-condition Paderborn set---and we label the last 20\% of measurement indices anomalous as late-life pseudo-labels. MFPT segments carry per-segment labels (baseline segments at 97.7\,kHz, fault sections at 48.8\,kHz). XJTU-SY \cite{wang2020} follows the last-20\% run-to-failure convention. All folds share the same encoder and calibration code; dataset sizes are equalized by deterministic window subsampling (seed fixed) where needed. Exact file selections are fixed in the released configuration: CWRU records 97--100 (normal) and 105, 106, 107, 108, 118, 130, 169, 185 (faults); Paderborn bearings B01--B04 (40 sampled measurements each, 8 windows per measurement); XJTU-SY bearings 1\_1, 1\_2, 1\_3, 2\_1, 3\_1; CARE assets 12, 16, 33, 35, 52; and MFPT baseline/fault segments; windowing, labeling, and injection code accompany this paper at \url{https://github.com/JibiaoZhu/crossdomain-conformal-calibration} (archived at \url{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23321794}). Throughout, ``anomaly'' denotes the operational or quality deviation defined by each protocol---a low removal-rate run, a status flag, a late-life window, or an injected perturbation---not necessarily a physical fault; F1's labels in particular mark process outliers rather than hardware failure.

\subsection{Encoder and Training}
\label{sec:encoder}
Scores come from a plain PatchTST-style reconstruction model \cite{patchtst2023}: patch length 64, stride 32, $d_{\mathrm{model}}=64$, 4 heads, 2 encoder layers, band-conditioned patch embeddings; the per-window score is the mean squared reconstruction error. Models train for 4000 steps with batch size 64 (AdamW, learning rate $5\times10^{-4}$, cosine schedule, AMP) on pooled source-normal windows; target scores are produced zero-shot. The step budget is fixed across folds and seeds with no validation-based early stopping, keeping encoders identical across folds; the terminal logged reconstruction loss is $\approx 0.015$ (mean over the final interval, no NaNs; per-fold logs released). No test-time adaptation, gating, or invariance regularizers are used in the main runs: earlier ablations showed their contributions were marginal ($\leq 2.2\%$ range-$F_1$ changes across all four Stage-0 folds) and they are reported as negative controls in Section~\ref{sec:neg-controls}.
